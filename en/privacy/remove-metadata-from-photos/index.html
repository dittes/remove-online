How to remove metadata (EXIF) from photos and documents

Photos can carry hidden data: where they were taken, when, and with which device. Here is how to strip it before you share a file.

Skip the steps and do it right here: Photo metadata removerOpen tool

Windows

  1. Right-click the photo and choose Properties.
  2. Open the Details tab and click Remove Properties and Personal Information.
  3. Select Create a copy with all possible properties removed and click OK.

Mac

  1. Open the photo in Preview.
  2. Choose Tools, then Show Inspector, and open the GPS tab.
  3. Click Remove Location Info. This removes the location only, not camera details.

In the Photos app you can also choose File, then Export, and untick Location Information before saving.

iPhone

  1. Open the photo in Photos and tap the share button.
  2. Tap Options at the top of the share sheet.
  3. Switch off Location (or All Photos Data) and share as usual.

Android

  1. Open the photo in Google Photos and swipe up to see its details.
  2. Tap Location and choose Remove location.

Office documents

In Word, Excel or PowerPoint choose File, Info, Check for Issues, Inspect Document, and then Remove All next to Document Properties and Personal Information.

Tip: Many social networks strip location data when you upload, but e-mail attachments and cloud links usually keep everything. Remove it before you send.

Questions

What exactly is in a photo’s metadata?

Typically date and time, camera or phone model, settings, and often GPS coordinates. Editing software can add its own name too.

Does a screenshot remove metadata?

A screenshot gets new metadata of its own and does not carry the original location. It is a quick workaround, but the image quality is lower.
